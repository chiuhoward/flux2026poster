  \vspace{-0.5cm}
  \begin{block}{What is DEE, and what does it ask?}
    \begin{columns}[t]
      \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
        \vspace{-0.4cm}
        \begin{block}{Why study this?}
          \vspace{-0.1cm}
          The HEALthy Brain and Child Development (HBCD) Study is a multisite,
          prospective longitudinal cohort recruited across 27 U.S. sites,
          designed to characterize brain, cognitive, and behavioral
          development from the prenatal period through early childhood
          \supercite{nelson2024hbcd}. We use HBCD Data Release 2.0 phenotype
          data to ask how caregiving and environmental context relate to
          early child development.

          \vspace{0.2cm}
          Brieant et al. \supercite{brieant2023characterizing} extracted ten
          adversity dimensions from a risk-only item battery in ABCD, later
          used by Beck et al. \supercite{beck2024dimensions} to predict
          brain-age gap. Neither study included any promotive or protective
          measure (caregiver warmth, cognitive stimulation, breastfeeding),
          so neither could test whether promotive and adversity content
          separate into distinct dimensions or collapse into a single
          bipolar continuum. \textbf{DEE puts both kinds of indicators in
          the same factor-analytic input matrix} and lets a genuinely
          Bayesian exploratory method \supercite{conti2014bayesian} decide
          -- the number of active factors and the item-to-factor allocation
          are inferred from the model's own posterior, never fixed by the
          analyst.
        \end{block}
      \end{column}

      \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
        \vspace{-0.4cm}
        \begin{block}{Two-stage design: discover, then confirm}
          \vspace{-0.1cm}
          Each developmental window (TP1: prenatal-9mo; TP2: 9-17mo) is
          modeled independently. Within each window, subjects are split
          once into an 80\% discovery sample (further divided into 5
          cross-validation folds, 4 MCMC chains per fold) and a permanent
          20\% confirmation holdout never touched during discovery
          \supercite{cudeckbrowne1983crossvalidation}. Rolling HBCD
          enrollment means TP2's usable N is far smaller than TP1's:

          \begin{center}
          \begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=2cm, auto]
            \tikzstyle{tp1block} = [rectangle, draw, fill=cardinalred,
              text width=4.4em, text centered, rounded corners,
              minimum height=3.4em, font=\small, text=white]
            \tikzstyle{tp2block} = [rectangle, draw, fill=paloaltogreen,
              text width=4.4em, text centered, rounded corners,
              minimum height=3.4em, font=\small, text=white]
            \tikzstyle{arrow} = [draw, thick, -latex']
            \tikzstyle{lbl} = [font=\footnotesize, text centered]

            \node [tp1block] (tp1_n) {\textbf{\normalsize N=4{,}117}};
            \node [tp1block, right of=tp1_n, xshift=2.9cm] (tp1_disc) {\textbf{\normalsize Discovery\\ n=3{,}304}};
            \node [tp1block, right of=tp1_disc, xshift=3.1cm] (tp1_hold) {\textbf{\normalsize Holdout\\ n=812}};

            \node [tp2block, below of=tp1_n, yshift=0.4cm] (tp2_n) {\textbf{\normalsize N=681}};
            \node [tp2block, right of=tp2_n, xshift=2.9cm] (tp2_disc) {\textbf{\normalsize Discovery\\ n=557}};
            \node [tp2block, right of=tp2_disc, xshift=3.1cm] (tp2_hold) {\textbf{\normalsize Holdout\\ n=123}};

            \node [font=\bfseries, left of=tp2_n, xshift=-1.4cm] {TP2};
          \end{tikzpicture}
          \end{center}
          \vspace{-0.1cm}
          17 candidate items at TP1, 15 at TP2 (caregiver-child relationship,
          breastfeeding, neighborhood/housing, discrimination, financial
          hardship, and language/cognitive stimulation measures).
          \textbf{H1} asks how many factors best describe this structure and
          whether it reproduces across folds and generalizes to the holdout;
          \textbf{H2}/\textbf{H3} ask whether promotive-dominant recovered
          dimensions predict more favorable outcomes, and whether that
          association is protectively moderated by adversity-dominant
          dimensions.
        \end{block}

  \begin{block}{H1: Structural Discovery}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    Neither window's recovered structure met the preregistered qualitative
    bar (broadly fold-stable, and fitting acceptably on the holdout):

    \begin{table}[ht]
      \centering
      \fontsize{9}{11}\selectfont
      \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l c c c c}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Window} & \textbf{Factors (M)} & \textbf{Gate: folds pass} & \textbf{CFI (holdout)} & \textbf{RMSEA} \\
        \midrule
        TP1 & 4 & 4/5 -- \textbf{FAIL} & 0.784 & 0.079 \\
        TP2 & 3 & 2/5 -- \textbf{FAIL} & 0.908 & 0.099 \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabularx}
    \end{table}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    All 5 folds must pass for a window's structure to enter the
    confirmatory family; scaled CFI/TLI fall well short of the
    conventional $\geq$0.95 threshold at both windows, and RMSEA exceeds
    the conventional $\leq$0.06 cutoff -- independent evidence, from a
    completely different angle (holdout generalization vs. cross-fold
    agreement), pointing the same direction.

    \vspace{0.15cm}
    \textbf{Promotive/adversity items: separate or bipolar?} Classifying
    every promotive/adversity item pair by whichever pattern has posterior
    probability $>$0.5:

    \begin{table}[ht]
      \centering
      \fontsize{9}{11}\selectfont
      \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l c c c c}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Window} & \textbf{Pairs} & \textbf{Bipolar} & \textbf{Separate} & \textbf{Neither} \\
        \midrule
        TP1 & 72 & 13 & 42 & 17 \\
        TP2 & 56 & 8 & 16 & 32 \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabularx}
    \end{table}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    Separation is more common than bipolarity at both windows, but neither
    pattern is close to universal -- most pairs don't cleanly fit either
    story.
  \end{block}
\end{column}

\separatorcolumn

\begin{column}{\colwidth}
  \begin{block}{H2 \& H3: Promotive Associations and Protective Moderation}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    Because both windows failed H1's reproducibility gate, the
    preregistered decision rule routes \emph{every} formed effect away
    from the confirmatory family:

    \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=cardinalred, colframe=cardinalred, width=\linewidth]
      \color{white}
      \centering
      \textbf{\Large Confirmatory family this run: 0 of 18 effects}\\
      \small (9 H2 main effects + 9 H3 interactions, RQ5--RQ22)
    \end{tcolorbox}

    \vspace{0.15cm}
    All 18 formed effects were still fit and reported descriptively
    (gated-exploratory set) -- every 95\% credible interval crosses zero,
    and every exploratory q-value is well above 0.05 (best case: TP1
    MAP-DB Temper Loss interaction, $q=0.222$). No pattern here licenses
    any confirmatory claim.

    \vspace{0.15cm}
    \textbf{Why the intervals are so wide.} TP1's four recovered dimensions
    are highly collinear (up to $r=0.955$ between two adversity-dominant
    factors); every adversity-dominant dimension enters the model
    simultaneously as a covariate, leaving little independent variation to
    attribute a unique main-effect slope to any one dimension. This is a
    property of genuinely correlated household-adversity domains, not an
    artifact of the estimation.

    \begin{table}[ht]
      \centering
      \fontsize{8}{10}\selectfont
      \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l l c c}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Outcome (window)} & \textbf{Hyp.} & \textbf{Post.\ mean [95\% CI]} & \textbf{Excl.\ q} \\
        \midrule
        SPM-2 (TP1) & H3 & 0.031 [-0.068, 0.129] & 0.241 \\
        MAP-DB Temper Loss (TP1) & H3 & 0.035 [-0.058, 0.128] & 0.222 \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabularx}
    \end{table}
    \vspace{-0.15cm}
    \tiny (Two of the 18 gated-exploratory effects, shown for illustration
    only -- full table in the reproducibility guide.)
  \end{block}
\end{column}

\begin{column}{\colwidth}
  \begin{block}{Supplementary Analyses (Not Preregistered)}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \textbf{Marginal (one-factor-at-a-time) models.} Dropping the
    collinear adversity covariates, two TP1 promotive main effects have
    credible intervals excluding zero:

    \begin{table}[ht]
      \centering
      \fontsize{8}{10}\selectfont
      \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l c c}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Outcome (TP1)} & \textbf{Post.\ mean [95\% CI]} & \textbf{q} \\
        \midrule
        SPM-2 & -0.150 [-0.241, -0.053] & 0.001 \\
        MAP-DB Temper Loss & -0.141 [-0.223, -0.054] & 0.001 \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabularx}
    \end{table}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    Hypothesis-generating only, not confirmatory -- a different
    model/estimand than H2, fit on a structure that already failed the
    whole-wave gate, and the two outcomes are not independent evidence
    ($r=0.437$, same-window caregiver report).

    \vspace{0.15cm}
    \textbf{7-item cross-timepoint check.} Restricting to the 7 items
    administered at both windows collapsed to a single active factor at
    each wave and made reproducibility \emph{worse}, not better (TP1 gate:
    1/5 vs. the full battery's 4/5; TP1 holdout CFI 0.266 vs. 0.784).
  \end{block}

  \vspace{-0.3cm}

  \begin{block}{Conclusions \& Future Directions}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \begin{itemize}
      \setlength\itemsep{0.15em}
      \item Neither developmental window's factor structure reproduced
        across folds or generalized cleanly to held-out participants this
        run.
      \item The confirmatory family was empty by design -- no promotive
        (H2) or protective (H3) association could be confirmed or
        disconfirmed, not evidence that such effects are absent.
      \item High inter-factor collinearity (TP1) limits how much
        independent signal any one recovered dimension can carry.
      \item \textbf{Next}: rerun without reverse-scoring any item (e.g.\
        CHAOS), since folding a reverse-scored adversity item into a
        ``promotive'' factor risks conflating absence-of-adversity with
        genuine promotive content; revisit as TP2's rolling-enrollment N
        grows.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Why study this?}
    \vspace{-0.1cm}
    \begin{itemize}
      \setlength\itemsep{0.15em}
      \item HBCD: multisite, prospective, prenatal through early
        childhood \supercite{nelsonIntroductionHEALthyBrain2024}.
      \item \textbf{Preregistered battery returned mostly null
        results.} Extended post-hoc with measures HBCD had actually
        collected (IBQR, GLED at TP2, ES4A/PSS-4/NBHSAF at TP2) --
        disclosed as a deviation.
    \end{itemize}

    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=coolgray, colframe=coolgray, width=\linewidth]
      \color{white}
      \textbf{Q1 (H1)}: does a low-dimensional promotive/adversity
      structure exist in HBCD's item battery, and does it reproduce
      across independent subsets of the same cohort?

      \vspace{0.1cm}
      \textbf{Q2 (H2/H3)}: if so, do promotive-dominant dimensions
      predict more favorable outcomes, and do adversity-dominant
      dimensions protectively moderate that association?
    \end{tcolorbox}
  \end{block}

\begin{column}{\colwidth}
  \begin{block}{H1: Structure Failed to Reproduce}

    \begin{table}[ht]
      \centering
      \fontsize{12}{13.2}\selectfont
      \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l c c c c}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Window} & \textbf{Factors (M)} & \textbf{Folds agreeing} & \textbf{CFI (holdout)} & \textbf{RMSEA} \\
        \midrule
        TP1 & 4 & 3/5 -- \textbf{FAIL} & 0.903 (scaled) & 0.048 \\
        TP2 & 4 & 1/5 -- \textbf{FAIL} & 0.798 & 0.107 \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabularx}
    \end{table}
    \vspace{-0.25cm}
    \begin{itemize}
      \setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
      \item We fit each window on 5 independently-resampled folds: TP1 agreed on only 3, TP2 on just 1.
      \item Held-out sample poorly fit at both timepoints (CFI $\geq$0.95, RMSEA $\leq$0.06 are the usual thresholds).
      \item Adding IBQR, GLED-TP2, and CHAOS post-preregistration changes item grouping but not instability.
    \end{itemize}

\begin{column}{\colwidth}
  \begin{block}{Supplementary (Not Preregistered): Confounds Explain Only Part of the Pattern}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \textbf{Marginal (one-factor-at-a-time) models} -- drop every
    other factor covariate, keep age/site only:

    \vspace{-0.5cm}
    \begin{figure}[ht]
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/marginal_effect_scatter_tp1.png}
    \end{figure}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \begin{itemize}
      \setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
      \item TP1's only 2 outcomes tested (SPM-2, MAP-DB) are both
        caregiver-reported on nearly the same sample ($r=0.437$).
      \item \textbf{Cross-timepoint check}: shared items are
        adversity-heavy, fit actually worse across chains and folds.
      \item \textbf{SES check}: maternal ed./income shrink but don't
        eliminate associations -- partial confounding; doesn't explain
        ADI/COI pairing.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}

  \vspace{-0.3cm}

  \begin{block}{Conclusions \& Future Directions}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \begin{itemize}
      \setlength\itemsep{0.15em}
      \item Neither window's structure reproduced across folds or
        holdout.
      \item Absence of evidence for H2/H3 association.
      \item TP1's factor collinearity limits  independent signal from any one factor.
      \item ADI/COI (neighborhood-level) correlate only $-0.24$ to
        $-0.35$ with the household-level items (breastfeeding,
        language exposure) they'd been bundled with -- distinguishable,
        not redundant.
      \item \textbf{Food for thought}: TP1's Early-resources,
        Nbhd.-safety, and Econ.-hardship factors intercorrelate up to
        0.96, yet ADI/COI stay distinguishable from household items --
        mixed evidence for Bronfenbrenner-style nested levels, worth
        designing a direct test for.
      \item \textbf{Next step}: cross-wave measurement-invariance test
        on shared items -- developmental change vs. instrument-switching.
      \item Revisit as TP2's rolling-enrollment N grows.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}
